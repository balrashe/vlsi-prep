01 / the basics

Brands will pay you to post about their app. That's the whole thing.

Tech UGC means a software company pays you to make short videos about their product and post them on TikTok and Instagram. You are not an influencer. You are making ads that don't look like ads.

You make a brand new account.

Not your personal page. A fresh account with a clean name, made for this. Nobody you know has to see it.

Followers do not matter.

Most tech brands have no follower minimum, no account age rule, and no engagement requirement. They care that you post, and that the videos perform.

You only need a phone.

No ring light, no mic, no camera. Phone footage is the point. It looks real, which is why it works.

You can run several brands at once.

Most of these deals are not exclusive. The creators earning the most are on more than one.

Why brands do this

Paid ads are expensive and people scroll past them. A creator posting from a normal account gets watched. So instead of one big ad, brands hire a group of creators to post a lot of small videos and see which ones take off.

who this is not for

This is not for you if you don't want to post consistently, don't want to be on camera, or want money without doing the work. Everything here takes real reps.

02 / the money

You get paid twice for the same video.

Almost every tech UGC deal pays in two parts. A flat rate just for making the video, and a bonus based on how many views it gets. The flat rate is your floor. The view bonus is where the real money is.

base pay

$25 to $60 per video, depending on format and track record.

view bonus

roughly $2 to $3 per 1,000 views on most deals.

together

one video that performs can outearn a week of flat-rate work.

Example only. Every brand structures this differently. Some pay a flat CPM, some pay fixed milestones, some do both. Always read the brief.

The trial is normal

Most good programs start you on a two-week paid trial at a lower rate. Hit their view target once and you move onto the long-term rate, which is often double. Do not skip trials. That is the door.

03 / the craft

There are only a few videos. Learn them and you can film anything.

Tech UGC looks varied but it isn't. Nearly every brief asks for one of these four. They pay differently, so know which one you're filming.

leads with your reaction

The reaction

You react to the tool doing something impressive. Your face, the screen, and a real reaction. Fastest to film, usually the lowest base rate.

leads with your argument

The talking head

You talk to camera about the tool and why it matters. Higher base rate because it takes a script and a real take.

leads with the screen

The demo

Screen recording of the tool working, with you on camera and your voice over the top. Strong when the result is the whole sell.

leads with the output

The result

Lead with the finished output, then show how it was made. Works when the product makes something people actually want.

length

30 to 60 seconds. Shorter is fine. Longer is usually worse.

face

Yes. Almost every tech UGC deal wants you on camera. This is not faceless content.

editing

CapCut or Edits by Instagram. Both free. You do not need anything else.

gear

Your phone. That's it.

04 / the craft

Your hook is most of your pay.

Since a big part of your money comes from views, and views come from people not scrolling past, the first three seconds decide what you earn. Not the editing. Not the lighting. The hook.

grabs the ear

Your spoken hook

The first line out of your mouth.

grabs the eye

Your text hook

The words on screen. They should not be the same sentence as what you say.

Do not invent hooks

Find videos in your niche that already got millions of views, take the structure of the hook, and swap in the product you're posting about. This is not cheating. It is what every creator earning real money is doing.

one format that works The countdown

A "top 5 worst [thing]" text hook, your face and voice delivering the list, and satisfying gameplay footage running underneath. The list keeps people waiting for number one. The gameplay keeps their eyes busy. The product lands somewhere in the middle. Same skeleton, endless topics.

why beginners never get paid

No real guidance, so they guess at everything and burn months.

They make up their own formats instead of using ones that already win.

They stop posting after two weeks.

They settle for one low deal instead of stacking better ones.

They never push for the deals that actually pay.

05 / do this now

A week from today you can be posting.

01

Make the account.

New TikTok and new Instagram. Clean handle, simple bio, profile photo. Nothing clever. It just has to look like a real person.

02

Study what wins.

Save twenty videos about AI tools that got big numbers. Write down the first line of each one. That list is your hook bank.

03

Film.

Pick a free AI tool, use it for real, and film three videos using three of your saved hooks. Same tool, three different openings.

04

Edit.

CapCut or Edits. Captions on, cut the dead air, keep it under a minute. Do not overthink this part.

05

Post and watch.

Post all three. Watch which one holds attention longest. That's your signal, not the like count.

06

Build the portfolio.

Now you have videos and real numbers. That is your portfolio. Brands do not want your follower count. They want proof you can make the thing.

07

Repeat.

Same loop, more reps. Volume is how you find your format, and your format is how you get paid.

06 / the hard part

Everything above is the easy half.

Making videos is learnable in a few weeks. The part that stops almost everyone is getting on good deals in the first place. That's where the money actually is, and it's the part nobody explains.

the trap

Public marketplaces

Everyone applies to the same listings. Low rates, no view bonuses, and you're competing with thousands of people.

SideShift · Billo · Insense
Trend · JoinBrands · Canva Creators

the move

Internal referrals

The best-paying programs never get posted publicly. They fill through people who already know the brand. No application pile, no negotiating, no waiting.

That difference is not small. It is the gap between $30 a video with no bonus, and a deal with a ladder that pays four figures on a single good post.

07 / straight answers

The six questions we get every day.

Do I need followers?

No. Most tech brands have no follower requirement at all.

Do I have to show my face?

Yes. Almost every deal wants you on camera. If that's a hard no, this isn't for you.

Do I need experience?

No. Nobody starts with experience. You build a portfolio by posting, not by waiting.

Do I have to buy the tool?

No. Brands give you access, and most of these tools have free plans anyway.

Is this legal?

Yes. You're a paid creator making sponsored content. Disclose partnerships the way the platform asks you to.

How fast can I start?

Today. Make the account tonight, film tomorrow.
